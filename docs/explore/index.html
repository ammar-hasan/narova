# Creative brief — The Rainmaker's Last Drop

Status: approved

## Intended effect

Wonder first, understanding second. A single drop feels precious rather than
cute. Its journey wakes a dormant greenhouse machine, and the final flower
opening feels earned by a visible chain of causes.

## Why 3D

The film depends on enclosure, depth, contact, transfer, and transformation
revealed through space. The camera moves from the whole greenhouse into the
contact zone, along the transmission, and finally upward with the flower.

## Representation and visual language

An invented glass-and-brass greenhouse machine occupies one continuous world.
Jade structure and plants establish life held in stillness; rain-glass cyan
isolates the tiny cause; aged brass carries the motion; dawn gold belongs only
to the bloom and final light. Materials are dimensional without pretending to
photorealism. The mechanism is authored choreography, not physical simulation.

## Temporal behavior

Stillness → fall → contact → geared transmission → cam lift → bloom. Camera
moves have perceptual jobs: establish the whole chain, witness contact, travel
with the transfer, reveal conversion into lift, and finish inside the bloom's
light. The flower remains closed until the final turn.

## Rejection criteria

- The drop reads as an ordinary opaque ball.
- The moving parts feel unrelated or respond before contact.
- Greenhouse architecture hides the contact or final bloom.
- Camera movement crosses solid geometry or loses the active subject.
- The final bloom is smaller or emotionally weaker than the setup.
- Captions obscure the mechanism or reintroduce a background card.

## Delivery target

Roughly 60 seconds, 16:9, cinematic 3D, restrained local narration, no app
chrome, word-synced captions without a background card, and a quiet original
sound bed.

# Creative brief — Narova skill reel

Status: approved

## Intent

Explain Narova's prompt → voice → synchronized motion pipeline in one compact,
first-party product reel. The purple field, lime/cyan/pink signals, large type,
and diagram-like scene changes make each production stage visually distinct.

## Approved pilot

The rendered hook and speech-synchronization scenes establish the intended
typography, palette, cue behavior, and diagram language. The published video is
the reviewed result.

## Rejection criteria

Reject if narration and reveals visibly disagree, captions obscure the scene,
the five stages become visually indistinguishable, text clips at 1280×720, or
the final encode loses spoken audio.

# Creative brief — one-command Narova demo

Status: approved

## Intent

Show the smallest honest Narova activation: one locally synthesized narrator,
two portable scenes, and restrained geometric motion that renders consistently
without browser-only effects.

## Approved pilot

The complete two-scene build is the pilot. Its centered play mark and staggered
dots demonstrate the portable visual vocabulary while keeping attention on the
editable command and project file.

## Rejection criteria

Reject if either renderer clips or misaligns the centered geometry, narration
or captions are absent, the dots do not reveal in sequence, or the output is
not a valid H.264/AAC MP4.
