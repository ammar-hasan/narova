# Narova 3D Production

Add a small expert lens to authored 3D work. Keep the `narova` skill in charge
of project authoring, assets, deterministic motion, rendering, review, release,
and delivery. This companion adds no renderer, physics engine, command, schema,
provider, dependency, or second workflow.

## Work lightly

1. **Give 3D a job.** State what authored 3D lets the viewer perceive that a
   simpler or different medium would not. If it adds no material value, use the
   better Narova path and stop using this companion.
2. **Form the scene intent.** Decide what the viewer should notice, understand,
   or feel; the spatial change that carries it; and the visual language that
   belongs to this work. Preserve the user's references and authorship.
3. **Find the material risk.** Choose at most the one or two areas most likely
   to weaken the idea. Read only their references:
   - read [space and appearance](references/space-and-appearance.md) when world
     structure, geometry/assets, materials, lighting, environment, depth, or
     compositing is uncertain;
   - read [camera and interaction](references/camera-and-interaction.md) when
     framing, paths, visibility, occlusion, contact, constraints, causality, or
     state changes matter; and
   - read [inspection](references/inspection.md) when a material claim needs
     stronger review, available scene state, uncertainty handling, or a stop
     decision.
   A straightforward scene may need none. Do not perform a full-manual pass.
4. **Direct through core Narova.** Use the least complex accepted 3D authoring
   surface that can express the intent. Build or source only what the scene
   needs. Follow the core skill for syntax, deterministic time, readiness,
   assets, proof branches, captions, sound, checks, and release.
5. **Inspect the actual work.** Review the decisive frames and motion, fix the
   material failure rather than decorating around it, and hand the project back
   to the normal Narova lifecycle.

## Keep confidence honest

Connect decisions to what was rendered or otherwise inspected. Render success
does not prove collision, physical simulation, target visibility, or hidden
scene state. Say what remains unverified; simplify the claim or stop when the
available evidence cannot support it. If repeated work needs inspection Narova
does not expose, identify a product-capability gap instead of adding more prose
or pretending the skill supplied machinery.

## Preserve creative range

Do not default the work to a palette, realism level, low-poly language, material
system, lighting rig, camera move, shot list, density, pacing, duration,
narration, caption style, transition, platform, or aspect ratio. Minimal and
rich scenes are both valid when intentional. Use hard constraints only where
user intent, compatibility, evidence, or visible correctness would otherwise
break.
