# Camera and interaction

Read this only when camera travel, visibility, occlusion, contact, constraints,
causality, or state change is a material risk.

## Give the camera an intention

For each meaningful move, identify the subject, the information or feeling the
move should reveal, and the state in which it should finish. Then choose any
path, framing, projection, lens character, height, distance, orientation, and
tempo that serves that intention.

Reason in the scene's actual space:

- keep the intended target visible when it must be understood;
- use occlusion deliberately for discovery, suspense, scale, or transition;
- preserve clearance from solid geometry unless crossing it is an authored
  visual device;
- check start, end, closest approach, turns, and occlusion changes—not only a
  smooth sampled path; and
- preserve continuity of position, screen direction, scale, and attention when
  continuity matters.

An orbit, dolly, pan, crane, handheld path, locked frame, cut, or impossible
graphic camera can all be right. Movement without a perceptual job is not
direction.

## Stage interaction as state change

Write the smallest causal account before animating a mechanism or contact:

```text
initial state -> cause -> contact or constraint -> response -> changed state
```

Name the objects and the observable condition at each arrow. At the decisive
instants, compare position, orientation, velocity, deformation, attachment,
clearance, and timing as applicable. Make the response consistent with the
scene's chosen scale and physical language.

Authored motion may be better than simulation when the goal is a controlled,
repeatable visual event. Simulation may be appropriate when emergent contact is
essential and the available project can make it deterministic and inspectable.
Never call keyframed plausibility a verified simulation, and never add a physics
system merely to make a simple motion sound rigorous.

## Protect causal legibility

Let the viewer see the relevant before, event, and after. A cut, label, sound,
or replay may support the interaction, but it cannot substitute for a missing
contact or contradictory state change. Watch for:

- interpenetration, hovering, sliding, tunneling, or response before contact;
- a hinge, cable, linkage, support, or path changing behavior without cause;
- speed, mass, force, or momentum cues that contradict the visible response;
- repeated footage presented as later causal progress; and
- camera motion that hides the instant required to understand the event.

When the event cannot be made coherent, simplify the mechanism, change the
representation honestly, or stop. Do not hide it with motion blur, a cut, text,
or decorative effects.
