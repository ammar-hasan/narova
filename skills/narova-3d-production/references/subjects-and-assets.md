# Subjects and assets

Read this only when a principal subject's final representation, production
path, rights, readiness, or movement is a material risk.

## Choose the received form

Separate a spatial blockout from the form the audience will actually receive.
Choose that final form for its intended reading, not because it is cheapest to
author:

- graphic or abstract form can make relationship, rhythm, scale, or emotion
  clearer than literal depiction;
- primitive, authored, or procedural geometry can be a complete intentional
  language, not merely a placeholder;
- a static model can carry recognizable silhouette, surface, and close detail;
- an articulated model can carry bodily performance and contact;
- captured or generated media can supply a subject that authored geometry
  cannot efficiently realize; and
- a mixed composite can let each layer do the work it does best.

This is not a closed catalog. Subject class does not prescribe realism or a
rig. Ask what must remain recognizable at delivery size, what articulation or
surface response the shot exposes, and whether the chosen form communicates
without the author's explanation.

## Route to available capabilities

Use the least complex available path that can honestly produce the accepted
form. Core Narova owns asset acquisition/import, provenance, scene assembly,
timing, and delivery. An independently available skill, generator, DCC,
conversion tool, or user-supplied asset may supply a specialist operation.

Route only what the form requires, for example:

```text
reference or source -> generate/acquire -> inspect -> refine/normalize
                    -> rig when needed -> animate when needed -> import
```

Skip irrelevant operations. Do not install a provider, invent credentials,
claim rights, or make one vendor or file format a Narova requirement. When no
available capability can realize the form, agree on another form, identify the
missing capability, or stop before full production.

## Inspect what will become structural

Inspect the selected asset in the views and motions that could disprove its
fitness. Depending on the work, check:

- item-level source, creator, rights, and downloaded bytes;
- silhouette and recognizable features at delivery scale;
- scale, orientation, pivot, bounds, support, and required clearances;
- topology, material slots, textures, transparency, and deformation quality;
- skeleton, skinning, joints, actions, loops, root motion, and attachments; and
- whether conversion, resize, optimization, or missing dependencies changed the
  accepted appearance.

Do not run this as a universal checklist. A graphic primitive needs no skeleton;
an articulated close subject may need nearly every motion check.

## Prevent placeholder promotion

A blockout may become final only through a deliberate visual decision. If the
artifact does not communicate the intended subject or world without a symbol
key, revise the representation or route another capability. Do not rescue an
unreadable form with labels, narration, polish, or a report explaining what it
was supposed to mean.
