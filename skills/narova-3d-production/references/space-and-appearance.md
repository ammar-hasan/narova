# Space and appearance

Read this only when the scene's spatial construction, form, assets, surface,
light, environment, or composite is a material risk. Use the questions that
change the work; skip the rest.

## Start from perception

Describe the scene as relationships rather than a list of objects:

- What is the viewer meant to find first, and what becomes clear later?
- Which separation, enclosure, distance, scale, alignment, or transformation
  carries the idea?
- What must feel supported, suspended, heavy, delicate, near, remote, inside,
  outside, hidden, or exposed?
- Which foreground, subject plane, background, negative space, or atmosphere
  makes those relationships legible?

Use a recognizable scale anchor when scale matters. Place every important form
in a world with intentional support, clearance, and depth; do not add layers or
objects merely to appear elaborate.

## Choose form deliberately

Use the simplest form that preserves the intended silhouette, articulation,
surface response, and close-up fidelity:

- primitives are excellent for abstraction, graphic worlds, blockers, and
  genuinely simple forms;
- custom or procedural geometry fits distinctive forms, repeated systems, and
  controllable detail; and
- sourced or generated models fit recognizable subjects whose shape, joints, or
  small features would be wasteful to fake.

Mix them freely. A context-rich brief should not collapse into placeholders,
but a minimal brief should not be burdened with ornamental assets. Inspect a
model's scale, pivot, orientation, articulation, topology, material slots, and
license before making it structural to the scene.

## Make surfaces describe the world

Material decisions should explain what things are and how they differ. Consider
roughness, reflectivity, transmission, thickness, texture scale, edge behavior,
wear, and variation only where they improve identity or focus. Avoid assigning
one undifferentiated finish to unrelated forms or using gloss as a synonym for
quality.

Light should reveal the chosen hierarchy and spatial change. Motivate it from
the scene's world or graphic logic, then use contrast, shadow, reflection,
environment response, fog, particles, or atmosphere when they clarify depth or
mood. There is no required key/fill/rim setup, light count, or realism level.

## Finish as one image

Judge the 3D and 2D layers together. Captions, labels, typography, browser
content, generated clips, and effects must share intentional scale, depth,
contrast, and focus with the world rather than appearing as an unrelated skin.

Stop when the intended perception is legible and complete. More geometry,
texture, light, atmosphere, or polish is not automatically better.
