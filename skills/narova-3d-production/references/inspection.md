# Inspection and confidence

Read this only when the visual premise, movement, a material spatial or physical
claim, or a stop decision needs stronger evidence.

## Separate the evidence states

Keep these distinctions explicit in reasoning and completion reports:

- **requested** — what the brief asks the work to communicate or do;
- **designed** — what the project intends to construct;
- **rendered** — what appears in the produced frames and motion;
- **inspected** — what was directly checked in the artifact or available state;
- **inferred** — what seems likely but was not directly established; and
- **unverified** — what the available product cannot currently prove.

Do not promote one state into another because a command succeeded or a frame
looks plausible.

## Prove the premise before scale

For risky work, separate two cheap questions:

1. **Visual premise:** show one delivery-scale artifact establishing the
   principal subjects, world, hierarchy, and visual language.
2. **Movement premise:** when motion or interaction carries the idea, show a
   short sample using the accepted representation in the intended scene.

Give perceptual reviewers only the artifact and the audience-level question.
Do not reveal the author's plan, symbolic mapping, rationale, condition, or run
report. Ask what they can recognize, where attention goes, what visibly
happens, and whether the result feels coherent and intentional. An explanation
cannot make an unreadable artifact pass.

A straightforward scene can collapse these proofs into normal production when
the same evidence is already directly observable. Do not create ceremonial
gates around low-risk work.

## Keep facts and perception separate

Use deterministic or structured evidence for available facts: file validity,
asset dimensions and rights records, transforms, bounds, camera samples, rig
and animation state, collision or constraint state, and build/release results.
Use artifact-only review for recognition, hierarchy, causal reading,
completeness, emotional effect, and taste.

Keep proxy names literal. A non-black or average-luminance result establishes
only that sampled pixels were not mostly black; frame difference or variance
establishes only sampled change; camera clearance establishes only the tested
distance; and a ray reaching a target establishes only that ray's geometric
path. None of these alone proves useful framing, subject visibility, readable
materials, room comprehension, route continuity, or narration-to-picture
correspondence. If direct artifact evidence or a capable perceiver is absent,
retain those audience-level questions as unverified even when every proxy
holds. Do not turn the uncertainty into a recommendation or creative gate.

An automated visual critic can flag issues or rank candidates, but keep it
advisory until its judgments have been compared with independent owner
judgments on this kind of task. Never pass the author's explanation to it as
evidence of what the image means.

Start with the smallest evidence set that could disprove the material claims:

- view the actual output at its intended size and pace;
- inspect the establishing, approach, contact, transition, closest-clearance,
  reveal, and final-state instants that matter to this scene;
- compare intended subject visibility, silhouette, depth, material response,
  light, and environment with the rendered result; and
- inspect available scene values, object transforms, camera samples,
  constraints, or other structured state when a hidden spatial or physical
  claim depends on them.

For DCC shots, inspect the exact source that will render. A master scene cannot
establish a shot file's collection, object, camera, or render-visibility state.
Choose the frames that matter to the current shot explicitly; the bounded
adapter can sample distant frames and render sparse proofs without selecting
them for you. Its camera transforms and deltas, render-enabled counts, light and
color-management values, world state, and requested object bounds remain
program facts. Compare those facts with actual proof frames; neither side alone
establishes useful framing, audience visibility, completeness, or smoothness.

When vision is unavailable, explicitly requested proof-pixel facts can expose
the decoded output's luma distribution, threshold fractions, saturation, alpha,
and channel ranges. Requested-object projection can expose where evaluated
bounds fall relative to the normalized camera frame. Read those values as
literal attributed information only. Near-dark pixels do not by themselves
mean a scene is too dark; bright pixels do not mean clipping is unwanted;
saturation does not prove colour separation; projected bounds do not prove
visible pixels, silhouette, recognition, hierarchy, or useful composition. The
companion provides no target band or recommendation. The authoring agent keeps
the uncertainty and decides what, if anything, follows.

Use core Narova's ordinary proof, shot, check, preview, and release evidence.
Do not invent an inspection artifact or claim the companion adds a validator.

## Diagnose before revising

Classify the material failure before changing the scene:

- **idea:** 3D or the chosen representation does not serve the intended change;
- **subject/world:** identity, relationships, scale, support, depth, form, or
  context are weak;
- **appearance:** material, light, environment, or composite obscures the world;
- **camera:** target, framing, path, clearance, occlusion, or continuity fails;
- **interaction:** cause, contact, constraint, trajectory, or resulting state
  contradicts the event; or
- **evidence:** the artifact or current Narova surface cannot establish the
  claimed correctness.

Fix the highest-level failing cause. Do not compensate for a broken interaction
with polish or for a weak idea with more geometry.

## Stop honestly

When direct evidence is unavailable, say what was and was not checked. Continue
only if a bounded visual claim is honest enough for the work. Otherwise simplify
the claim, change the representation, or stop.

If the accepted form needs an unavailable generation, refinement, rigging,
animation, simulation, or inspection operation, name that capability gap. If
several projects repeatedly need the same missing Narova surface, record a
separate product change. Do not expand this skill into a fictional tool
interface.
